# Start here: prepare a credible organisational commitment

**Version 1.0 · 19 November 2026 · MIT · For policy, leadership and communications professionals**

You do not need to write code or understand JSON. Use ordinary documents or tables. The technical schemas are for integrations that specifically request them.

## 1 Describe the decision

Tell the assistant which organisation or business unit is involved, who the commitment is for, what needs to improve and who can approve it. Include relevant countries, services and employment relationships where known. Unknowns are acceptable: name them so the right person can resolve them.

**Why:** the same wording may be appropriate for one service and misleading for another. Neither a brand value nor another organisation's pledge establishes your authority or obligations.

## 2 Gather what you have

Provide approved priorities and commitments, relevant policy, evidence of current delivery, feedback and available capacity. Identify actual document dates and approval status. State privacy restrictions before supplying any personal information. Do not upload confidential employee cases into public or unapproved systems.

A writing-style guide, benchmark and complete set of metrics are optional for starting useful preparatory work. If the scope or authority is unknown, establish those before proposing an organisational decision. Missing baseline data can become a measurement task rather than an invented result.

## 3 Ask for one stage

Open [the prompt library](prompts/README.md). Copy the appropriate prompt, replace bracketed inputs with your information and say which facts are unknown. Start at intake for a new project; a bounded review may start with the relevant stage, with its limits stated.

Use this brief in ordinary English:

> Help prepare a draft disability-inclusion commitment for [organisation and scope]. The intended audience is [audience]. Our approved priority is [priority, or unknown]. Here are our dated documents and delivery evidence: [references]. Our confirmed decision authority and limits are [details, or unknown]. Start with [stage]. Give the result in a readable table, identify evidence and open decisions, and do not invent approval or performance.

## 4 Review the result

For each substantive statement ask: is this a legal duty, an approved promise, demonstrated practice, approved future work or a proposal? Is its source clear? Who is accountable? What action, evidence, participation and review are needed? Is the intended customer or employee outcome clear?

For possible legal obligations use [the public source map](PUBLIC_SOURCE_MAP.md) to identify questions. Repository summaries cannot decide legal applicability. An unresolved legal question need not stop an unrelated safe improvement or independent drafting.

## 5 Plan delivery and measurement

Use a readable table with the headings in [the delivery template](templates/commitment-delivery-record.yaml): statement; approval; actions; human owners; capacity; baseline; measure; target; participation; reporting; review; decisions. You may use the headings without producing YAML. Use the structured template only if a system needs it.

Separate activity (what was done), output (what was produced), outcome (what changed for people) and impact (a broader effect supported by appropriate evidence). Do not substitute training attendance or a scan score for equitable opportunity or satisfaction.

## 6 Obtain approval before publication

An actual authorised human must approve the exact version and destination. Record the real decision reference and date in the organisation's appropriate system. Do not infer approval from a completed template, an assistant's assurance result or a schema check. Outstanding authority, evidence or participation issues limit affected claims/actions and must remain visible.

## 7 Review honestly

Compare progress with the approved scope, baseline and measure. Use not_measurable or not_reported when evidence cannot support a result. Record missed commitments and changed context; do not quietly replace a baseline to erase a gap. Obtain approval for changes or new public claims.

See [the fictional worked example](examples/WORKED-EXAMPLE.md) before trying the method. It contains no genuine approval, participant evidence or organisational achievement.
