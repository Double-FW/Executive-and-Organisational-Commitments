Executive statements, speeches, commitments, policies and diversity strategies all need to set out clearly what the objective is for the organisation in terms of its approach and perspective of disability inclusion. These are the foundations for, and should be reflective of, an organisation’s brands values and they should align with its mission statements, principles and all other communications that set the cultural tone and objective for an organisation. These things are important because they are where we find the permissions for accessibility as both a cultural and strategic objective. Our digital accessibility policy is about ensuring digital products, services and digital environments are accessible to everyone by removing barriers and facilitating inclusion. Digital Accessibility can relate to: websites, mobile applications, TV platform applications, video games, voice skills, XR experiences, digital learning experiences, online live events, content re-purposed from broadcast TV, off-schedule AV content, content published on social media platforms, provision and publication of information and organisational practices – the way we do things in relation to audiences. Being inclusive and accessible to disabled people is an integral part of the Diversity Strategy. Accessibility is therefore a key objective and ambition to ensure that our products and services deliver value for everyone. Disabled people are a diverse group that makes up over 20% of the population. This policy relates to anyone who has an impairment or condition that requires special consideration. For disabled people accessibility is vital for rights of access, social inclusion, participation and opportunity. As such we design products and services in a way that aims to provide disabled people with an inclusive and comparable experience that is equivalent to that enjoyed by non-disabled people. To ensure this is both achieved and maintained, accessibility is part of a programme of continuous research and improvement. Digital accessibility will also be reported at Board level through an Executive Accessibility Champion. The Accessibility Commitments are also central to the way our subsidiaries and joint venture partnerships develop new products and services. Accessibility will be considered from the start when developing new products and services, or partnership proposals, ensuring accessibility is built and iterated in a way that is both scalable and sustainable. Accessibility is recognised as a joint responsibility of the Product, Design and Editorial teams in respect to their function. The digital UX design system, will have accessibility built-in to every pattern and resource it provides, including designed experiences for both sighted and vision impaired users with support for voice, keyboard, point or touch control input where appropriate. All projects will aim to support platform level accessibility features and other assistive technologies through compliance with, and not limited to, WCAG AA (mobile and web) and Annex 1 of theEuropean Accessibility Act (EAA) for all products and services including commercial ventures. A Digital Accessibility Team, supplier or dedicated function will provide, guidelines, training, skills development, advice, tools and resources. These resources enable and support inclusive design, editorial and technical delivery of digital products and could be delivered by AI if this is proven to be effective. An Accessibility Champions Network will ensure every project will have at least one person representing the voice of disabled users. Disabled participants will be sought in all design and audience research programmes to ensure they have a voice in all consumer research.
